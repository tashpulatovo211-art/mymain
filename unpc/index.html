Uzbekistan National Policy Competition

Every Sunday in Tashkent, high school students are drawn into teams, given one real policy problem and an hour to solve it. University professors judge, and the winning resolution is sent to the Oliy Majlis.

Register for Sunday See how a tournament runs
Case 1 of 4 First tournament, 4 October 2026

National identity and values among young people in a globalized world

How can Uzbekistan strengthen national identity and cultural values among young people without limiting their openness to global culture and international exchange?

The Uzbek language, a foreign language and a practical skill

How can an effective system be created so that young people, while keeping the Uzbek language and national identity, acquire at least one foreign language and one practical profession or professional skill?

Young people’s responsibility for a healthy society

Identify the social behavior or harmful habit that poses the greatest challenge to young people today, and propose a practical policy solution to address it.

Each team picks the habit it argues about.

A young person’s own responsibility for national values

Part one: in an increasingly globalized world, what responsibility does an individual young person have in preserving national culture, language, traditions and historical memory? Part two: how can the state encourage young people to preserve national values without forcing them to?

Teams answer both parts.

A tournament takes a little over two hours.

From the welcome to the last certificate it runs 124 to 139 minutes, and almost half of that is the hour of preparation.

0 30 60 90 120 139 min

  1. 20 to 30 min

    Welcome and guest speaker

    The organizers explain the rules, then a guest speaker talks to the students.

  2. 60 min

    Preparation

    Teams are drawn on the spot and get their case. There is an optional coffee break inside the hour.

  3. 24 min a room

    Presentations and questions

    Two teams per room. Each presents for 7 minutes, then answers the judges for 5.

  4. 5 to 10 min

    Written resolution

    Each team writes up its policy and hands it in, with any change the questions led to.

  5. 15 min

    Award ceremony

    The winning team and the best speaker from each room get certificates and prizes. The best speaker can come from the losing team.

Judges score each team out of 100.

Two teams work on the same case in each room, and the judges score both on one sheet. The sheet starts on a tie, so move the sliders to see who wins.
