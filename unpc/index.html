Next tournament This Sunday

Students write policy. The best goes to parliament.

The Uzbekistan National Policy Competition is a nonprofit tournament for high school students in Tashkent, run by students and held every Sunday.

Register for Sunday How a Sunday works

  1. UNPC has run every Sunday in Tashkent since 4 October 2026. Each dot here is one of the more than 500 students it has engaged so far.

  2. Students are drawn into teams of three or four on the spot. Both teams in a room get the same real policy problem and one hour to prepare. Each team presents for 7 minutes, then answers the judges’ questions for 5.

  3. Judges are local university professors. Each block of seats is one criterion on their score sheet, sized by its points. Their score sheet has these six criteria.

    • Understanding of the problem 15
    • Quality of the solution 20
    • Argument and evidence 20
    • Answers to questions 15
    • Communication and teamwork 15
    • Written resolution 15

  4. After the questions, each team writes and hands in a short resolution: the policy, stated clearly, with the changes the judges’ questions led to.

  5. The winning resolution is sent to the Oliy Majlis, Uzbekistan’s parliament. After the first tournament it went to Bobur Bekmurodov, a member of the Legislative Chamber.

    These are the four cases of the first tournament. The briefs were written in Uzbek; these are the English translations. None needed knowledge of tax, constitutional law or macroeconomics.

    Case 1 of 44 October 2026

    National identity and values among young people in a globalized world

    “How can Uzbekistan strengthen national identity and cultural values among young people without limiting their openness to global culture and international exchange?”

    Case 2 of 44 October 2026

    The Uzbek language, a foreign language and a practical skill

    “How can an effective system be created so that young people, while keeping the Uzbek language and national identity, acquire at least one foreign language and one practical profession or professional skill?”

    Case 3 of 44 October 2026

    Young people’s responsibility for a healthy society

    “Identify the social behavior or harmful habit that poses the greatest challenge to young people today, and propose a practical policy solution to address it.”

    Each team picks the habit it argues about.

    Case 4 of 44 October 2026

    A young person’s own responsibility for national values

    “Part one: in an increasingly globalized world, what responsibility does an individual young person have in preserving national culture, language, traditions and historical memory?

    Part two: how can the state encourage young people to preserve national values without forcing them to?”

    Teams answer both parts.

Annex A

How a room is scored

Each team is scored out of 100 on six criteria. Move the marks to score two teams; the sheet breaks a tie by the real rule.

If the totals tie, the higher score for quality of the solution wins.

The best speaker is picked separately and can come from the losing team.
